Is the Shuffle Really Random?

This page uses the same basic deal structure as the current Griffin House Rules: a 43-card deck with the Bird (Rook) and Red 2 included, 9 cards dealt to each of four players, and 7 cards left in the nest.

The real-life odds

20.93%One specific Bird to one player
20.93%The other Bird to one player
3.98%Both Birds to one player

For any particular player, 9 of the 43 cards go into that player's hand. So either specific Bird has a 9/43 = 20.93% chance of landing there. Getting both has a probability of (9/43) × (8/42) = 3.98%.

The small differences are exactly what you expect from random sampling. Over a finite number of hands, nobody will receive the Birds exactly the same percentage of the time. As the number of hands increases, the results tend to move closer to the mathematical odds.

What this means at the table

A player can get the Bird several hands in a row, or go a long stretch without seeing it. That does not by itself indicate that the shuffle is favoring or punishing anyone. Random deals naturally produce streaks and uneven short-term results.

The important comparison is the long-run distribution. In this 100,000-hand test, every player stayed very close to the expected 20.93% chance for each specific Bird, and every player stayed close to the expected 3.98% chance of receiving both.
